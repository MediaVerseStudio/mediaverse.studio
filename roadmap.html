Where it is, and where it is going

As of version 0.9.1, the second public beta (26 November 2026).

Working today

  • Windows: library, playlists, playback, Now Playing, mini player
  • CD ripping with AccurateRip
  • Fix tags, covers, lyrics, the Lyric AI and the defect scan
  • Organizing files by a naming rule, with undo
  • Android player, Android Auto, and keeping chosen Jellyfin music on the phone
  • Samsung TV app (build it yourself)
  • Jellyfin: shared playlists and flagged songs

In beta testing

  • Phone and computer in step over Wi-Fi new
  • Sending new music from the phone home new
  • Profiles on the TV and phone new
  • Pictures inside every song
  • Casting to Google Cast and DLNA devices
  • The Jellyfin server as the whole computer library
  • Recognizing songs by their sound (AcoustID)
  • WASAPI output and ReplayGain tags

Next

  • Profiles in the Windows program
  • The Jellyfin plugin as a download
  • A setup guide for first-time users
  • Signed Windows installers
  • Samsung's store beta, then Google Play's closed test
  • Google TV, from the Android app

Planned and in progress

  • Linux, starting with an AppImage (in development)
  • Podcasts, kept apart from your music
  • ASIO output; levelling on phone and TV
  • Scrobbling, star ratings, skins and add-ons
  • Microsoft Store, Google Play and Samsung's TV store

Help us test it, and help us get it into the stores

MediaVerse Studio works on real computers, phones and TVs, and is ready for more eyes. If you love your music collection, we would like you in the beta.

Who we need

  • Windows 11, with a music folder (CDs to rip are a bonus)
  • Android phones, and Android Auto in the car
  • Samsung smart TVs
  • Google TV, Chromecast or a Cast speaker
  • A Jellyfin server at home
  • Linux users, for when that build exists

What it takes

  • Install it, listen the way you normally do, and tell us what happens
  • No tests to run by rote
  • Back up your music before using tools that change files
  • Nothing is collected from your devices: you tell us in your own words

What you get

  • New builds first
  • A say in what gets built next
  • A free program that has been tried on your library

Why now? Google requires new developer accounts like ours to run a closed test with at least 12 testers, opted in for 14 days in a row, before an app may be published on Google Play. The Microsoft Store and Samsung's TV store have reviews of their own. Testers are how this free project reaches the stores and more platforms.
